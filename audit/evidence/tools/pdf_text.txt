What to have ready
 A working title. It can be provisional; choose wording that helps you recognize the project.
A reader description: age or life stage, interests, and what they need from the book.
Two or three sentences about the book idea and the experience you want readers to have.
A monthly AI spending limit and a maximum estimated cost for any one request, both in USD.
In the browser
Open Workspace, find Start a new book, and fill in your name, project name, working book title, intended
reader, and idea. Select an available AI provider and set both spending limits. Choose Create book and save
idea.
WHAT IS SAVED
The application creates a project, a title, and your human-written idea note. It also records your selected
provider and hard spending limits. This step does not make an AI request.
Know the two containers
Project
The workspace for one book or a related set of books. Its provider and spending limits
apply to work inside it.
Title
The individual book you open to develop its concept, plan, chapters, verification, and
release packet.
IF YOU CANNOT START
If no provider appears, ask the system operator to configure a ready provider with model pricing. The
browser cannot create a book without one. Do not enter an API key into a book form.

3 / 8
Follow the concept path
Reader brief
Under Book authoring > Develop your idea, generate an audience brief. Check that it
describes the intended reader accurately.
Options
Generate concept options. These are suggestions, not commitments. Choose an idea
that fits your reader and purpose.
Concept brief
Develop the chosen option into a fuller concept brief. Read the complete result and use
Edit in browser if you need to correct it.
Check
Run Check concept completeness. An advisory scorecard can help you notice gaps, but it
does not approve the idea.
Human approval
Open the concept brief under Awaiting acceptance. Read its content, provenance,
findings, blockers, and conditions before accepting it.
Questions to ask before accepting
 Can I explain this book in one or two plain sentences?
Is the intended reader specific enough to guide writing choices without excluding people unnecessarily?
Does the concept promise an experience or benefit the book can reasonably deliver?
For fiction: are the story premise and reader appeal clear? For nonfiction: is the reader problem and
practical value clear?
DECISION POINT
Acceptance is a human gate. If the concept is weak, revise the experimental brief and review the new version.
When the concept is accepted, you can request a drafting brief and move into planning.

4 / 8
Build the plan in order
Positioning
Define the reader, the book's promise, its tone, and how it should be distinguished.
Review and accept it, then advance to Validated when the gate allows.
Book brief
Set the scope, reader outcomes or story experience, voice, boundaries, and success
criteria. Review and accept it.
Outline
Map the full journey of the book. Check the sequence, gaps, repetition, and whether the
promised length and scope feel workable.
Chapter cards
Create a card for each chapter or entry. Each card should tell the writer what that part
must accomplish and how it connects to the whole.
Use Book authoring > Plan the book. Open every generated item in Awaiting acceptance; a generated plan is
not automatically the approved plan. When the required items are accepted, use Advance to planned.
A useful planning review
 Compare every chapter card with the accepted outline. Does each chapter have a distinct job?
Check the audience and tone across positioning, brief, outline, and cards. They should point to the same
book.
Keep any claims, source needs, or rights questions visible for later verification.
IF A STAGE BUTTON IS BLOCKED
Open the title's planning and Awaiting acceptance sections. The system may be waiting for an accepted item
or another required condition. Resolve the named requirement, then try the stage button again.

5 / 8
The chapter cycle
1. Draft
Choose Start drafting stage, then Draft one chapter. Enter the chapter number that
matches an accepted card.
2. Read
Open the experimental draft. Check the chapter's purpose, flow, tone, reader
usefulness, and alignment with the card.
3. Analyze
Run continuity or developmental analysis when useful. Other editorial passes can focus
on voice, line, copy, reader experience, or consistency.
4. Revise
Use Edit in browser for a manual revision, or request an AI revision if you choose. The
original stays available; each new version remains experimental.
5. Accept
Open the chosen draft or revision under Awaiting acceptance. Review its content,
provenance, findings, blockers, and any conditions before accepting it.
6. Continue
A chapter summary can carry continuity context into later work. Then repeat the cycle
for the next chapter.
What acceptance means
Acceptance puts the chosen version into the manuscript's accepted chapter set. It does not mean every
source, claim, or quotation is verified, or that the book is ready for KDP. Record unresolved conditions instead
of hiding them.
EXAMPLE CONDITION
If a chapter contains [SCRIPTURE NEEDED], leave it visible. Acceptance can carry a Scripture verification
condition, but the reference must later be supplied and checked by a person. Never invent a quotation to
clear the marker.

6 / 8
What needs checking?
Scripture
Scan accepted chapters for placeholders. A person supplies an exact reference and
checks it against the project's source policy.
Sources
Check cited material and the exact statement it supports. Record a traceable evidence
reference.
Claims
Flag statements that need factual support, qualification, or revision.
Research
Check research-derived points and whether the evidence applies to the reader and
context.
Rights
Record third-party text, images, or other material and the evidence for permitted use or
a decision not to use it.
On the title page, use Workflow actions > Verification to scan for placeholders or add a verification flag or
rights record. Open each pending item from the title page or attention queue. The review page asks for a
named reviewer, rationale, and evidence where needed.
A SCAN IS NOT VERIFICATION
The scan only finds placeholders in accepted chapters. It does not choose a reference, edit manuscript text,
or mark anything verified. A recorded human decision must match the actual book content and evidence.
What a blocker tells you
A blocker is a visible reason the manuscript or release candidate is not ready. It can be a missing verification,
unresolved approval condition, rights issue, or another preflight concern. Read the exact blocker before
deciding what to change.

7 / 8
From chapters to review packet
Build
Choose Create Markdown build. The system assembles accepted chapters and records
the included assets, file hashes, and build status.
Inspect
Read the build and its blockers. A blocked draft build can still help you review the
manuscript; blocked does not mean release-ready.
Candidate
Create a release candidate from a selected build. It freezes a particular manuscript build
for preflight review.
Packet
Export the review packet. It gathers the candidate, provenance, blockers, and other
evidence for a human review.
Decision
The release review page shows the artefact, provenance, findings, conditions, blockers,
and consequences before approval, hold, or rejection.
Before a positive release decision
 All intended chapters are accepted and the build contains the right versions in the right order.
Scripture, source, claim, research, and rights questions have human evidence and decisions.
No release blockers or unresolved approval conditions remain.
The review packet matches the build and is understood by the person making the decision.
PUBLICATION BOUNDARY
This Workspace prepares and reviews the book. It does not upload to KDP or publish for you. Final KDP action
remains manual and human-controlled.

Review page

If something is not moving
 Look for a named blocker or missing accepted artefact before repeating an action.
A failed or unclear AI request may still have incurred usage. Inspect its job and cost before deciding to
retry.
If generation is unavailable, check whether your project has a selected ready provider, configured pricing,
and a hard budget. Ask the system operator for configuration help.
If a review is unavailable, confirm the item is still experimental and its source content has not changed.
YOUR REPEATABLE ROUTINE
Open the title -> inspect Attention -> do one action -> read its result -> review or revise -> accept only when
ready -> check remaining blockers. The system records state; you supply judgment.
The in-app Help page has the shorter version of this journey. On staging, the Workspace is available only to permitted
Tailscale users. Your system operator manages access and provider configuration; creators work through the browser.
